The financial picture

Sector context

Three licensed mobile network operators serve a population of roughly 35 million under CST regulation, all three listed on the Tadawul Main Market with full IFRS-KSA disclosure and quarterly reporting. This is an oligopoly with high fixed-asset intensity, so the analytical question is not top-line growth (aggregate revenue compounded at 6.2% a year from FY2018 to FY2025 with no year of contraction) but whether capital deployed into 5G, fibre and adjacent digital businesses earns above its cost. Aggregate EBITDA margin fell from 38.1% in FY2018 to 33.3% in FY2025, a 4.8 point compression driven by device cost of sales rising from 7.2% to 15.7% of revenue as the mix shifted toward handset and equipment resale, which carries structurally lower margin than connectivity.

Executive snapshot · FY2025
Industry aggregate ROIC 12.6% vs WACC 5.9%: spread +6.7pt

The Saudi telecom industry generated SAR 108.4B of revenue in FY2025 on an aggregate ROIC of 12.6% against an aggregate WACC of 5.9%, a +6.7 point spread that makes the sector, in aggregate, comfortably value-creating. That aggregate hides the only comparison that matters: stc contributes 72% of industry revenue and 79% of industry NOPAT, so the sector number is very close to stc's own. Read company by company, the industry splits three ways: stc creates value at scale with deteriorating cash conversion, Mobily creates value on an improving trend with the cleanest cash conversion in the industry, and Zain KSA destroys it.

Sector risks

Sector-wide

Device cost of sales has risen from 7.2% to 15.7% of aggregate revenue since FY2018. If the mix shift continues at the same pace, a further 3–4 points of EBITDA margin is at risk across the industry regardless of connectivity pricing.

High likelihood
Sector-wide

Aggregate receivables stood at 131 days of sales in FY2025 versus 133 in FY2018 (flat at industry level), but the dispersion has widened sharply, with Zain KSA deteriorating from 93 to 168 days. Industry-level stability is masking a single-company problem.

Already occurring

What the evidence shows

Findings · as at 07 August 2026

Where the evidence points, stated as findings rather than as a call. Mobily is the only company in the industry whose ROIC−WACC spread is both positive and still widening while leverage falls: ROIC rose from 3.4% to 11.0% across the window as debt-to-equity fell from 0.94x to 0.28x. stc is the largest value-creator in absolute terms at a +5.2 point spread, but returned SAR 21.4B of dividends against SAR 6.5B of free cash flow in FY2025, a cover of 0.30x. Zain KSA has cleared its cost of capital in one year out of eight, even after shedding 8,069 towers to a PIF-led consortium in 2023, a disposal that removed the most capital-intensive part of its asset base without lifting returns. No investment recommendation is offered on this site.

Commentary

From 1 January 2018 to 6 August 2026, Mobily was the strongest performer in the industry, rising +281% from SAR 16.10 to SAR 61.30, an annualised +16.8%. Zain KSA was the weakest at -15% in total, or -1.8% a year. The TASI returned +52% over the same period (+5.0% annualised), so on price alone Mobily and stc outperformed the index.

Drawdowns separate the industry as sharply as returns do. Mobily fell 31% peak-to-trough at its worst point against 57% for Zain KSA, on annualised volatility of 28% and 31% respectively. The single best calendar year in the industry was Mobily in 2019 at +51%; the worst was Zain KSA in 2018 at -30%.

These are price series only. They say nothing about whether the underlying businesses earned their cost of capital over the same period; that comparison is on the Value Creation tab, and the two do not line up neatly.

What the shares show

Who leads. stc holds 71.8% of industry revenue in FY2025, roughly 4.0 times the next operator. That position has barely moved: it was 72.8% in FY2018, so 8 years have cost it 1.1 points. Over a window in which the industry's combined revenue grew 52%, the order never changed and was never in question.

Where the movement is. Mobily is the only operator to have gained ground, from 16.6% to 18.1%, a gain of 1.5 points. It is recent and it is accelerating: 1.1 points of it has come in the last three years alone. Both stc and Zain KSA gave up ground over the window, 1.1 points and 0.4 points respectively.

Why. Mobily grew revenue 66% across the window against 52% for the industry, and did so while carrying the heaviest reinvestment in the group: capital expenditure of 16.4% of revenue in FY2025, against 15.2% at stc and 4.5% at Zain KSA. It also runs the widest EBITDA margin at 39.8%. Growing, reinvesting and converting at the same time is the combination that moves share in a network business. Zain KSA is the counter-example: at 4.5% of revenue its investment rate is a fraction of its peers', which makes share gains structurally difficult whatever the commercial effort behind them.

What it means for the investment question. The rate of change is the point. Mobily has taken 1.5 points in 8 years; even at the faster pace of the last three, closing the gap to stc is a matter of decades, not cycles. Share here is close to fixed, and an investment case that rests on one operator taking the market from another is not supported by anything in this series. Every one of the three grew revenue over the window. What separates them is not how much they sell but what they earn on the capital they commit to selling it, which is the comparison on the Value Creation tab and the one that actually divides this industry.
